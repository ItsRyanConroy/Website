FPV Drone

The Problem

I wanted a fully custom-built 5-inch freestyle quad rather than a pre-built RTF drone, to learn how the frame, flight-controller stack, motors, and digital video system actually integrate. I did not want to fly something someone else assembled. That meant researching and hand-selecting every subsystem individually instead of buying a bundled kit.

Build

  • GEPRC GEP-MK5 5" freestyle frame (30.5×30.5mm FC mount, 20×20mm VTX mount, 16×16mm motor mounts)
  • DolphinRC F405 V3 60A flight controller / ESC stack
  • MEPS Neon 2207 1950KV motors
  • Walksnail Avatar HD Pro Kit, dual antenna
  • Walksnail Avatar Goggles X
  • RadioMaster RP1 V2 ELRS receiver, flown with a RadioMaster Zorro controller
  • CNHL Black Series V2.0 1300mAh 6S batteries, charged on a HOTA D6 Pro

Design Decisions

Walksnail/DJI O4 Video System

Choosing a digital video ecosystem meant weighing Walksnail against DJI's O4 system. After digging into both against primary manufacturer sources rather than just going with community consensus, Walksnail's Avatar HD Pro kit won out for this build.

VTX Power and Data Routing

The VTX is wired to a 10V regulated rail, with data carried over TX2/RX2 to the flight controller. This keeps the video link's power source clean and isolated from the noisier rails elsewhere on the stack.

Challenges

Custom Antenna Mount

The GEP-MK5 frame didn't have a native mounting solution for dual Walksnail antennas, so I sourced a purpose-made mount (the "GEPRC Mark5 Walksnail antenna mount + XT60" from Cults3D) rather than improvising something less clean.

High Thermal Mass Soldering

Used a tin-both-then-reflow technique with leaded 63/37 solder for stack connections, tinning both surfaces first, then reflowing them together, for cleaner joints on small pads than trying to feed solder in cold.

Ground Station V6

The Problem

The Formula SAE team's timing gates need a pit-side reciever that can pick up lap/split data over the air and get it in front of the team in real time. This means picking up ESP-NOW packets, fowarding them to a Raspberry Pi 5 for display and logging, and doing it reliably enough to trust over a full test day. Roughly 200m of range, no drift in timestamps, and no dropped packets nobody notices until after the run.

Requirements

The board needed to satisfy a few core functions to be useful trackside:

ID Requirement Status
ELEC-1 Recieve timing gate data over ESP-NOW at up to ~200m range Met
ELEC-2 Foward data to a Raspberry Pi 5 over UART Met
ELEC-3 Log data locally to a microSD card as a backup to the Pi-side record Met
ELEC-4 Run data entirely from USB-C power, with a fallback power path if needed Met
ELEC-5 Fit the board outline to the Raspberry Pi's form factor without overhanging its USB/Ethernet connector stack Met

Design Decisions

ESP32-S3 as the Radio and Control Core

The board is built around an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N8 (8MB flash, no PSRAM, built-in antenna). It receives timing packets over ESP-NOW and bridges them to the Pi over UART, with three handshake/interrupt lines: two ready signals on IO37 and IO38, and a GATE interrupt on IO14, wired to Pi header pins 11, 13, and 31 respectively.

Power Path: Buck Regulator + USB-C Priority Mux

A TPS56339 buck converter steps 5V down to 3.3V for the ESP32 and peripherals. A TPS2116 power mux handles switchover with USB-C given priority (threshold set around 4.13V via a 47kΩ divider), so the board can run standalone off USB-C for bench testing without needing the full harness connected. Each mux input gets its own PTC fuse (Bourns MF-SM100/33-2, 1.1A hold / 2.2A trip) for independent fault isolation per source.

Layout: Switching Section Polygon Pours

Layout feedback on the buck converter section pushed toward polygon pours instead of traced connections, to minimize switching-induced voltage drop.

Status LEDs

The board carries six status LEDs for at-a-glance state feedback. Current-limiting resistors were sized per rail rather than using one blanket value across the board — 100Ω for the LEDs fed from the 3.3V rail, and 200Ω for the one LED fed from 5V, keeping forward current consistent across LEDs pulling from different supply voltages.

Challenges

Power Budget Correction

Initial worst-case sizing assumed ~900mA draw at 5V. Closer analysis of the actual 3.3V rail draw showed it was closer to ~500mA.

BOOT Pin Reliability

The ESP32-S3 has an internal pull-up on its BOOT pin, which is technically enough to meet spec. I added an external 10kΩ pull-up (R28) anyway. A floating or weakly-pulled BOOT pin is a known cause of flaky power-on behavior on ESP32 boards. It is cheap insurance against an intermittent failure that would otherwise be maddening to debug in the field.

RF Range and Antenna Keepout

The board needs roughly 200m of ESP-NOW range to be usable trackside, so the WROOM-1's antenna keepout area wasn't an afterthought, it shaped where nearby copper, components, and even the board edge itself could go. Antenna keepouts on integrated-antenna modules like the WROOM-1 typically mean no ground pour, no components, and no routed copper directly beneath or immediately surrounding the antenna trace. Even a nearby ground fill can detune the antenna and quietly cut down real-world range.

Status

The board is currently in production for the 26–27 competition year; bring-up will begin in September.
